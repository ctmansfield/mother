# MOTHER database privacy assessment

**MSE-DATABASE-PRIVACY-20261121-01 r1 · Assessment and design**

**Decision.** Treat the authoritative longitudinal archive and its derived outputs as sensitive, linkable health data. Keep the synthetic-only boundary. The current destination is **NOT READY**, and no reviewed store or projection has evidence supporting release as de-identified. Select separate custody for identity, exact evidence and analytical outputs before handling personal records. Even with that separation, a recognizable single-subject trace can disclose identity and health information.

## Unauthorized access scenarios

| Access lost | Disclosure that remains possible | Control that changes the outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One DB dump | A full journal or PF-001 dump contains raw bytes and is an exact-record exposure. A derived-view-only dump can still link longitudinal windows by time, stable tokens, rare activity, and correlation to a named account or known test cohort. A main application dump can expose free text, account-linked reminders, behavior or memory independently. | Use separate stores and distinct keys/roles. Minimize each analytical projection, with no base-table grant or dump capability for the analytical identity. Encrypted volumes protect stolen media while locked. They do not protect a plaintext dump. |
| Database plus artifacts, backups, logs or receipts | Digests, UUIDs, sequence, timestamps, manifest relations and duplicated originals enable joins. A forgotten unencrypted backup can undo otherwise sound primary-store controls. Repeated exports can reveal changes by differencing. | Encrypt backup, transport and staging; segregate restore permissions and vault backups; keep exact internal digests out of any reduced projection. Review disclosure across releases, not one export in isolation. |
| Application credential or service compromise | Reads or writes allowed by that role, including bypassing application-only filters, bulk queries and observation of decrypted active data. A shared service identity or key increases scope. A snapshot grant cannot prevent external copies after legitimate release. | Per-purpose short-lived grants, read-only view roles, independent write/restore identities, bounded queries and approvals for export. Bind scope server-side, audit response sizes, revoke grants and invalidate served views. Protect full-access service accounts separately. |
| Host administrator, root or key custody compromise | A live compromised host can observe process memory, tamper with code, collect decrypted records or exercise accessible keys. Combined vault and evidence access can reverse aliases. | Move the identity vault and its key custody to a distinct administrative boundary, and keep key operations auditable. Separate hosts or equivalent isolation reduce common compromise paths. Root on a host processing plaintext remains an explicit residual risk. |
| External correlating dataset | Known appointment/workout times, start-stop patterns, device/app labels or rare HR/SDNN trajectories can identify a single subject despite random aliases. Deterministic hashes of predictable IDs permit guess-and-compare; even high-entropy stable IDs are join keys if observed elsewhere. | Suppress or generalize time/context in purpose-limited projections, use per-domain random aliases, test linkage against plausible synthetic auxiliary knowledge, and keep exact single-subject traces in a controlled setting. Stable time offsets preserve intervals and motifs; common anchors and repeated releases can undo them. Binning harms temporal inference and can leave rare cells. Do not claim a public release is safe without evaluation. |

The project has demonstrated that exact artifact and receipt/revision digests are designed to match across approved components. It has also demonstrated in synthetic tests that reopened state can be resolved to exact observations. These are integrity and linkage facts. The attack scenarios above are reasoned exposure models. No unauthorized production access, personal re-identification or external-dataset matching was performed.

## Decision-ready separation

1. **Identity and grant vault.** Use one narrowly scoped identity service to bind real account, source enrollment and authorized purpose to random internal tokens. Store the reverse mapping in a separately administered encrypted vault with named recovery custodians. Issue different random subject and source aliases for different analytical domains or releases. Only preserve longitudinal joins where necessary for the approved purpose. Use restricted lookups and audit mapping access. Plain hashes of an account, email, name or source ID are unsuitable. If an internal keyed lookup is necessary, use independent domain keys and keep lookup capability inside the vault. Avoid exposing stable internal IDs or content hashes through views.
2. **Exact archive.** Keep originals, immutable revisions, precise clocks, raw lexemes, scoped tombstones and canonical source linkage under a private evidence identity. Use dedicated ingest/write, recovery and review roles. Apply authenticated encryption and documented per-domain key access to archives, backups and transport. Prefer separate data keys and wrapping keys across identity, evidence and backup domains; define activation, rotation, revocation, emergency recovery and recovery-key access separately. Plan tested rotation with readback and historical verification. Rotation alone leaves old plaintext, logs and backup copies accessible wherever they already exist. [NIST SP 800-57 Part 1](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.800-57pt1r5)
3. **Purpose-scoped query and projection.** Give the model a bounded snapshot service and give analysts least-privilege, row- and column-scoped views or physically separate exports. Default exclude native UUID, bundle/source names, exact addresses, artifacts, immutable digests, raw provenance text and unrelated context. In reduced projections, use purpose-specific aliases, justified time granularity/relative time and rare-event suppression. Version each transformation with disclosure scope, source cutoff, rounding/generalization loss and utility validation. For MSE replay that needs exact spacing, retain exact data inside the controlled evidence-to-model boundary. Never adjust authoritative timestamps/labels in place. Require explicit analysis of linkability, repeated releases and what an external recipient knows. [NIST SP 800-188](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/188/final) · [HHS OCR de-identification guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/de-identification/index.html)
4. **Restore and lifecycle.** Establish limits and custody for phone originals, pending and rejected payloads, artifacts, journal/revisions, snapshots, model outputs, audit trails and backups. Keep source deletion, revocation of access, and an owner erasure action as distinct events. Source deletions suppress current serving and invalidate dependent snapshots and derived results under the cutoff contract. Erasure needs an authorized policy for originals and history, indexes/embeddings, cache/temp/log copies, exports and backup expiration or sanitization. On restore, replay the authoritative suppression/erasure ledger before releasing any views. Record what could not be recalled. Cryptographic erasure is conditional on all relevant copies being encrypted with keys that can actually be destroyed, including wrapping/backup copies. Confirm applicable organizational and legal retention requirements separately. [NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/88/r2/final)
5. **HR/SDNN fit.** Preserve HR in bpm and vendor SDNN in ms with distinct physiology labels. Keep exact values and available time/location metadata semantics in the archive, and distinguish source, collector receipt, MOTHER receive and commit clocks. No inference about raw RR, RMSSD, sensor method, device, calibration or permission coverage follows from these files. Preserve finite source-only values, record binary64 conversion and range losses, and withhold unknown-derivation HR from native reference input. A synthetic archive candidate can use PF-001 and local single-writer custody. Choosing SQLite for a real destination requires storage/lock/durability and paired restore evidence; considering PostgreSQL requires an explicit compatible schema, constraint, security and migration decision. Engine availability alone leaves both choices open.

HHS guidance describes formal methods in a specified HIPAA context. It is used here as a technical reference to risks around dates and identifiers. This assessment does not determine legal applicability, certify either method, or promise anonymity.

Three gates were relayed to ATLAS as the minimum for candidate review:

1. **Trusted grant and narrow authority.** Derive subject/source, allowed purpose/role and generation from a trusted grant registry. Matching two caller-supplied labels provides no authority. Scope the analytical entry and resolver to an explicit field allowlist, refuse denied requests before exact data or mappings are opened, and prove lower-level APIs cannot hand analytical consumers exact records or the vault through the advertised boundary. A path or in-process role check running under the same UID cannot isolate a caller with direct filesystem or Python execution access. 2. **Revocation and restore.** Bind an opaque random analytical handle to scope, purpose and generation, with no stable source/revision digest embedded in it. Keep current revocation authority outside the projection or mapping backup being restored. A restored stale handle must fail against the live generation, including missing registry or vault state. A projection-only restore must exclude originals, vault mappings and their recovery material. Treat same-UID or host/root access and access to usable recovery keys as stronger exposure scenarios. Use vetted platform controls; no cryptography or keys were configured here.
3. **Linkage and public shape.** Require a bounded fixed export schema and coarse allowlisted losses or errors. Keep native and subject/source IDs, bundle and device labels, exact timestamps, paths, raw bytes and stable hashes in restricted custody. Suppressed groups must not reappear in counts, loss pointers, logs or errors. Relative buckets, output order, rare windows and changing or repeated release populations can still identify a single subject from auxiliary timing knowledge. A positive analytical utility test does not certify de-identification.

Include only approved classes and fields. Record delivery separately from creation. | Export authority is distinct from viewing, ingestion and backup. Verify cross-scope and expired grants, partial export failure and cleanup, and restricted retention of the exact transfer receipt. |
| Evidence writer and verifier | One dedicated writer commits bounded artifacts and acceptance state; a verifier reads exact receipt/revision bindings under an independent approved path. | Keep the archive authoritative and preserve input versions and clocks. A successful write or digest alone does not prove a normalized commit. A filesystem copy by an administrator exposes the entire readable SQLite file. |
| Backup operator, key custodian and recovery approver | Separate responsibilities where the threat model calls for it. The backup role takes a coherent generation, the custodian controls required decryption and recovery material, and the approver selects an isolated restore for review before publication. | An operator with both backup contents and usable keys can read exact evidence. Keep key recovery and break-glass access explicit, attributable, time-bounded and separately logged. |
| Host, hypervisor and service administrators | Name who can control the physical host, guest volumes, snapshots, process memory, service identities and configuration. | A guest or container can separate ordinary service access. Administrators of the controlling host or hypervisor may still inspect or roll back unlocked guests. If the design must withstand that compromise, put authoritative keys or freshness state under independent administration and validate that boundary. |

Audit should record actor, operation, scope reference, reason, authorization outcome, time, and outcome/receipt or export reference in a separately governed sink. Keep original bytes, raw identifiers, credentials and key material out of routine logs. Specify who can see exact audit references. Decide what stops when identity, key or audit custody is unavailable. Define a break-glass path that preserves attribution and review.
