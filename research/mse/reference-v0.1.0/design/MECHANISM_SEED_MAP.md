# Proposed mechanism seed map — MSE v0.1.0

These two bounded motifs are proposed research-map content, not existing implemented inference or findings about this individual. E1 remains the fixed project premise; the edges below have separate empirical support and scope. Each row should become a revisioned claim with participant roles, compartments, conditions and evidence—not an unconditional arrow. An established reaction does not establish its current flux, contribution to a sensor pattern, or therapeutic usefulness.

## Motif 1 — Postmeal glucose transport and disposal

Boundary: recorded meal → glucose appearance → circulating glucose → tissue transport/phosphorylation. MSE can describe an observed glucose response when a glucose source exists; separating intestinal appearance, hepatic production and disposal requires additional information. Glucose and HRV do not identify the insulin-signaling pathway's activity.

| Proposed claim | Concrete biological participants and scope | Prerequisites and missing measurements |
|---|---|---|
| Insulin signaling can alter cell-surface glucose transport capacity | **Insulin → AKT kinase → TBC1D4/AS160 → Rab trafficking → GLUT4** is the bounded experimental seed. Add an insulin-receptor node as its upstream dependency, with receptor abundance/engagement unknown in this person. Sano's phosphorylation-mutant experiment directly tested the AS160–GLUT4 link in **3T3-L1 adipocytes**; extension to this person's skeletal-muscle response needs its own evidence. [1] | Receptor availability, phosphorylation state, downstream competence, surface GLUT4, glucose delivery/perfusion and concentration gradient. Missing here: contemporaneous insulin, tissue signaling assays and tissue-specific uptake. Insulin concentration alone would still not measure receptor engagement or downstream response. |
| Signaling/transport capacity is distinct from glucose carbon fate | **AKT** is an enzyme; **AS160** is a Rab GTPase-activating protein; **GLUT4** is a transporter. Phosphorylation uses ATP as phosphate donor, while Rab proteins have a GTP/GDP cycle. These are separate biochemical roles, not a claim that GLUT4 itself hydrolyzes ATP to transport glucose. The mutation experiment establishes a signaling/trafficking relationship in its assay. [1] | A falling glucose curve does not prove oxidation: storage and other disposal routes remain possible. ATP availability, trafficking and downstream metabolic flux require distinct observations. No tissue enzyme activity, surface transporter abundance or tracer flux is available from HR/SDNN. |
| Observed meal response is a balance, not an identified disposal mechanism | Model equation: `VG·dG/dt = meal appearance + endogenous production − disposal − renal loss`. This is a bookkeeping constraint. The original minimal-model experiment used measured insulin input and controlled glucose administration. [2] | Known meal timing/composition improves context; insulin/C-peptide can constrain some alternatives; appropriately designed tracer studies distinguish appearance/production/disposal more directly. Absent these, publish a glucose-response phenotype with competing explanations, not “insulin resistance” or “mitochondrial impairment.” |

**Individual interpretation:** a larger excursion after poor sleep or inactivity is a candidate context association. It does not select the INSR–AKT–GLUT4 explanation over altered absorption, hepatic production, perfusion or measurement effects. Experimental evidence for a pathway belongs to the mechanism map; the person's pathway activity remains unknown until supported by discriminating measurements.

## Motif 2 — Cross-feeding, butyrate and epithelial response

Boundary: substrate and microbial partners → fermentation intermediates → butyrate → epithelial transport, oxidation and signaling → ecological feedback. Keep luminal, intracellular and circulating compartments separate.

| Proposed claim | Concrete biological participants and scope | Prerequisites and missing measurements |
|---|---|---|
| Strain-specific cross-feeding can support butyrate production | In anaerobic coculture, **Bifidobacterium adolescentis L2-32** with **Eubacterium hallii L2-7** supported use of lactate/acetate generated during starch utilization; other partner/substrate combinations used carbohydrate breakdown products. Preserve the paper's strain names and its pH conditions. [3] | Substrate reaches the compartment; compatible organisms coexist; uptake/enzymes are active; pH, redox, transit and competing consumers permit flux. Taxonomic abundance alone does not establish any of these. Missing: strain/function data, quantitative metabolites and compartmental flux. |
| The terminal butyrate reaction depends on the enzymatic route | **Butyryl-CoA:acetate CoA-transferase** transfers CoA from butyryl-CoA to acetate, yielding butyrate and acetyl-CoA. A **Roseburia sp. A2-183** gene product was tested experimentally. **CoA** is the acyl-carrier coenzyme; acetate is a cosubstrate, not a generic positive ecological label. [4] | Requires the particular enzyme, substrates and physiological reaction conditions. Do not attach this pathway to every butyrate-producing taxon or assume the Roseburia enzyme experiment directly characterizes E. hallii. Gene presence is capacity; isotope or rate measurements are needed for flux. |
| Transport, oxidation and receptor response are separate branches | Keep lumen-to-cell transport/activation as explicit unresolved nodes in this small seed. Human **ACADS** oxidizes intracellular butanoyl-CoA using enzyme-bound **FAD** and electron-transfer flavoprotein; verified **UniProt:P16219**. **GPR43/FFA2** and **GPR41/FFA3** ligand activation belongs to separate receptor-binding/signaling claims. [5,6] | Compartment transport, competing substrates, intracellular activation, redox/cofactor cycling and receptor availability matter. Neither stool butyrate nor a gene count measures intracellular oxidation, occupancy or signaling. FAD dependence is not evidence of cofactor deficiency or supplement efficacy. |
| Host response can change the microbial niche conditionally | In studied mouse systems, reduced epithelial **PPARγ** signaling altered **NOS2/nitrate** regulation; oxygen-related effects also depended on inflammatory context. Colonocyte metabolism, oxygen/nitrate availability and bacterial respiratory fitness close the feedback loop. [7] | Preserve the AND-condition linking reduced PPARγ signaling and the relevant inflammatory challenge for the oxygenation result. Do not generalize mouse challenge conditions to an individual human or substitute a wearable “inflammation score.” |

**Proposed dynamic template:** population growth, washout, phage lysis, compartment transport, and receptor surface/internalized/desensitized pools are separate dynamical variables with unknown parameters, not fixed edges. This template is not experimentally established activity in this person. Promote reviewed claims through pinned mechanism snapshots; keep unidentified fluxes, receptor states and parameters null.
